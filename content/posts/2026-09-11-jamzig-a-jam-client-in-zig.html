--- title: JamZig⚡, a JAM client in Zig date: 2026-09-11 subtitle: Why Zig, what Milestone 1 meant, what is next ---

The posts on this site stop around 2015. A lot happened since. The biggest thing on my desk today is JamZig⚡, a JAM client written in Zig. I am the sole developer. This is a short account of what it is and where it stands.

JAM in one paragraph

JAM, the Join-Accumulate Machine, is the protocol Gavin Wood designed as the successor to the Polkadot relay chain. The Web3 Foundation runs a prize for independent implementations, split into tiers by language category. Twenty-two teams entered. JamZig⚡ is the only Zig implementation among them.

Why Zig

I have been in Rust for years and I still like it. For a protocol client the appeal of Zig is that nothing is hidden. Every allocation takes an allocator you handed over yourself. There is no hidden control flow, no destructor running behind your back, no macro layer between you and the code. When a state transition has to match a reference byte for byte, that plainness is worth a lot. Comptime replaces most of what I would reach for generics or code generation in other languages. And the language is small enough that one person can hold it in their head while also holding a protocol in their head. That was the bet, and it still feels right.

What is in it

  • A RISC-V virtual machine, the PVM, which runs services and their refine and accumulate logic.
  • The cryptography the protocol asks for: Bandersnatch ring VRFs, Ed25519, BLS12-381, Blake2b, Keccak-256.
  • Reed-Solomon erasure coding and the Merkle structures for availability and state.
  • Block authoring, validation, and the state transition function, checked against the official test vectors.

Milestone 1

Milestone 1 of the prize is about conformance. The state transition function has to match the reference implementation under fuzzing: ten runs of one hundred thousand steps each, no divergence. JamZig⚡ was the first to pass in the systems-language category. After the M1 interview I am the JAM Prize M1 candidate for it.

Passing conformance is less a finish line than a change of state. Before it, every bug could be anywhere. After it, the core of the client is known to agree with everyone else, and work can move outward from there.

What is next

The specification keeps moving, so keeping up with it is a constant background task. The prize has further milestones past conformance, and those are the road ahead. Conformance releases and test exports for other teams are published in the org, so other implementations can check themselves against the same runs.

The project lives at jamzig.dev, the code at github.com/jamzig, and the running commentary on X at @jamzig_dev. If you are building a JAM client in another language, say hello.
